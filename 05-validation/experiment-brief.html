Choose your method — and why not the others

A single onboarding-screen change randomizes cleanly at the individual trial-user level, and the signal (reaching a modeling output) appears within the first session. That makes a standard A/B test the cleanest, most logical tool — the deck's rule is "if A/B is enough, don't overcomplicate it."

Why not the rest: no scarce-conversion pressure → no MAB; no marketplace / pricing / network effects → no Geo or Switchback; the signal is fast → no Holdout needed now (though a Holdout is the right follow-up to measure the retention tail).

Define the experiment

Objective (→ North Star)
Lift activated trial users (import data + reach first modeling output) by removing the connect-a-bank wall.
Primary metric
% of new trial users reaching their first modeling output within 7 days.
Success threshold
Significant (p < 0.05) ≥ 5pp absolute lift vs control, read at a pre-set date ~6 weeks out. Extend rather than peek early.
Guardrail (must not break)
Trial→Paid conversion of the sample-data cohort must not significantly drop — guards the vanity-activation trap.
3

What we're testing

Current experience
Users hit "Connect your bank"; if not ready, they stall and never reach a modeling output.
The single change
Add one option — "Explore with sample data" — so they reach a first modeling output now, then connect real data.
Target segment
New trial signups in the reverse trial, randomized 50/50 at signup.
4

Predict the outcome

Predicted
First-modeling-output rate rises ≥ 5pp vs control (the hard gate is gone), while trial→paid conversion holds flat.
✓ If successful
  • Ship the sample-data on-ramp.
  • Run a Holdout to confirm the retention / ARR tail over weeks.
  • Open the separate pricing / paywall experiment — the real 2% ceiling from M4.
✗ If unsuccessful
  • No lift → the gate isn't the blocker; instrument Get-Started to find the true drop-off.
  • Lift but conversion falls → we activated low-intent users; qualify the sample path or pivot to the pricing experiment.
