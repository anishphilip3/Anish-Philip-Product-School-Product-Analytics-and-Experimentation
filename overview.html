Context

What is FinWise?

FinWise is a B2B SaaS product that helps small businesses manage their finances — connect accounts, model cash flow, forecast, and collaborate with their accountant. It uses a product-led growth (PLG) model with a reverse trial: users get full access for 14 days, then drop to a free tier.

The company sits at $10M ARR with a healthy top-of-funnel. But there's a problem downstream: only 2% of trial users convert to paid, and that number hasn't moved in 13 months. Meanwhile, 60% of paying customers churn within a year. The front door works — the living room doesn't.

The exercise

What this project covers

This certification project applies the full Product Experimentation framework to FinWise across six modules. Each module tackles one layer of the growth problem — and each one hands the next its reason to exist:

M1 Bet→ M2 Solution→ M3 Mechanic→ M4 Signals→ M5 Validation→ M6 Model

The through-line: Bet (the leak is conversion + retention, not acquisition) → Solution (get users to the Aha faster) → Mechanic (make the value a weekly habit) → Signals (the data says the 2% is a structural ceiling) → Validation (test the activation on-ramp; read results honestly) → Model (fix the pricing ceiling the signals exposed).

Module 1

The Bet — Ignite a PLG Motion

Question: Where should FinWise place its growth bet?

Hypothesis: If we add an in-trial milestone guiding users to FinWise's core value action (connect data → first modeling output), then trial→paid conversion rises ~1–2pp (from 2% toward 3–4%), because most users never hit the Aha moment before the paywall.

The bet: A Collaboration growth loop, not acquisition. Make FinWise multiplayer — owner invites accountant, they manage books together, more seats join, FinWise becomes the system of record, the team hits limits and upgrades, and each collaborator seeds a new workspace.

What we deliberately skip: Content loops and referral loops — those feed a top-of-funnel that already doesn't convert.

Module 2

The Solution — Acquisition & Activation

Question: What is FinWise's Aha moment, and how do we get more users to it?

The Aha moment: A user connects their data and sees their first personalized financial snapshot — one plain-language insight about their money. Not a feature: a realization.

The problem: Only ~39% of trial users ever import data, and that ceiling has sat at ~40% for 13 months. The connect-a-bank step is the wall.

The fix: A sample-data on-ramp. Users who aren't ready to connect a real bank can explore with sample data and reach a modeling output immediately. Five screens, three to value: Welcome → Connect (bank or sample data) → Snapshot (the Aha) → Invite → Success.

Module 3

The Mechanic — Retention & Engagement

Question: How do we turn activation into a lasting habit?

"Money Momentum" — a forgiving weekly streak with value milestones. Weekly notification triggers a review; sustained use unlocks deeper reports (4-week: Monthly Trend, 12-week: Quarterly Forecast, 52-week: Year in Review). A grace week auto-banks monthly — miss a week, no reset.

Why this design: Rejected leaderboards (financial data is private — ranking users on money behavior is a trust violation) and points/badges (extrinsic rewards decouple from real value). Money Momentum works via loss aversion (protect the streak), owner identity (each week builds "my money picture"), and goal gradient ("3 more weeks to Quarterly Forecast").

Module 4

The Signals — Data & Analytics

Question: What does the data actually say about FinWise's growth problem?

The uncomfortable finding: Modeling Usage nearly doubled (index 100 → 187) while conversion never moved (r ≈ 0.24, not significant). The 2% cap is structural — a pricing/paywall problem, not an activation-depth problem.

Five metrics across 13 months revealed three distinct patterns:

  • Ceiling: Trial→Paid conversion (flat at ~2%) and Import Usage % (~40%)
  • Growth: Modeling Usage % (31% → 58%, the one metric rising)
  • Cliff / Slow Leak: Session duration dropped sharply; sessions/user U-shaped

This split the strategy into two experiments: (A) lift Import Usage to grow the activation pool, and (B) restructure pricing to break the conversion ceiling.

Module 5

The Validation — Experimentation Methods

Question: How do we test the activation fix with rigor?

A/B test — 50/50 split. If we add an "Explore with sample data" path on the Get-Started screen, then more trial users reach their first modeling output. Primary metric: % reaching first modeling output within 7 days. Success: significant (p < 0.05), ≥5pp absolute lift.

Why A/B: Chose over MAB (no scarce-conversion pressure), Holdout (signal appears in first session), Geo-Test (no marketplace effects), and Switchback (no temporal carry-over).

Guardrail: Trial→paid conversion must not significantly drop.

Read date: ~6 weeks, pre-set — no peeking.

Module 6

The Model — Pricing & Monetization

Question: How should FinWise restructure pricing to break the 2% ceiling?

Recommendation: Reverse-trial freemium. Day 1–14: full premium access. Day 15+: permanent free tier (import + basic modeling). Paywall at the feature boundary, not a time boundary — users upgrade because they need more, not because a clock ran out.

Stage: Revenue Expansion (Stage 2) — $10M ARR proves value creation is done. The lever is the model and paywall placement.

Signal to watch: Conversions should cluster tightly at the paywall boundary. ARPU must not decline. Free-tier users must show higher Import Usage % than current trial users.

Reflection

What I learned

One friction

I kept reaching for pricing as the fix. The data forced me to see it was an activation problem first — the connect-a-bank wall was the real friction. Pricing came after, and only because the data proved the 2% was structural.

One Aha

Realizing the 2% conversion number was a symptom, not the disease. When modeling usage nearly doubled and conversion didn't budge, the funnel diagnosis changed my entire strategy — from "fix activation depth" to "fix the paywall."

Key takeaways

Experimentation is a system, not a tactic. Stage before method. Model follows value. The read date set up front, and knowing when NOT to change the price, were the lessons that stuck. Every module handed the next its reason to exist.
