The Company

What is FinWise?

FinWise is a B2B SaaS product that helps small businesses manage their finances — connect accounts, model cash flow, forecast, and collaborate with their accountant. It uses a product-led growth model with a reverse trial: full access for 14 days, then a permanent free tier.

The top-of-funnel is healthy. The problem is downstream: most trial users never experience FinWise's core value before the paywall hits, and paid users churn because the product stops feeling new after week one. The front door works — the living room doesn't.

01
M1 · The Bet

Collaboration growth loop

FinWise is stuck in single-player mode. The bet: make it multiplayer. Owner invites accountant → they manage books together → more seats join → FinWise becomes the system of record → upgrades → each collaborator seeds a new workspace.

Hypothesis: An in-trial milestone guiding users to their first modeling output lifts trial→paid conversion ~1–2pp.

M2 · The Solution

Sample-data on-ramp

Only ~39% of trial users ever import data. The fix: let users explore with sample data so they reach a modeling output — the Aha moment — immediately. Five screens, three to value.

Aha moment: A user connects data and sees their first personalized financial snapshot — one plain-language insight about their money.
Prototype → Presentation →
03
M3 · The Mechanic

"Money Momentum"

A forgiving weekly streak with value milestones. Weekly notification triggers a review; sustained use unlocks deeper reports. Grace week auto-banks monthly — miss a week, no reset. No leaderboards (privacy), no points (extrinsic decay).

Works via loss aversion, owner identity, and goal gradient — aligned to the weekly cadence of real financial activity.
Wireframe → Scorecard → Presentation →
04
M4 · The Signals

The 2% ceiling is structural

Modeling Usage nearly doubled (index 100 → 187) while conversion never moved (r ≈ 0.24, not significant). Five metrics, three patterns — Ceiling, Growth, Cliff/Slow-Leak — revealed the 2% is a paywall problem, not activation-depth.

This split the strategy: (A) lift Import Usage to grow the pool, (B) restructure pricing to break the ceiling.
Dashboard → Presentation →

M5 · The Validation

A/B test the on-ramp

If we add an "Explore with sample data" path on Get-Started, more trial users reach their first modeling output. Clean per-user randomization, 50/50 split, ~6-week read date pre-set with no peeking.

Reverse-trial freemium

Move from a time-limited trial to a reverse-trial freemium model. The paywall sits at the feature boundary, not a time boundary — users upgrade because they need more, not because a clock ran out.

Reflection

What I learned

Biggest friction

I kept reaching for pricing as the fix. The data forced me to see it was an activation problem first — the connect-a-bank wall was the real friction. Pricing came after, and only because the data proved the 2% was structural.

Key Aha moment

Realizing the 2% conversion number was a symptom, not the disease. When modeling usage nearly doubled and conversion didn't budge, the funnel diagnosis changed my entire strategy.

What stuck

Experimentation is a system, not a tactic. Stage before method. Model follows value. The read date set up front, and knowing when NOT to change the price, were the lessons that stuck.
