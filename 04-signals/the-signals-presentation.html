The Signals · 04
The test that matters

Does modeling usage predict conversion?

  • Indexed to 100 at Oct '23 so both series share one honest axis — no dual-scale distortion.
  • Modeling climbs to ~187 (nearly 2× its base) while conversion never leaves the 97–108 band.
  • They move independently — deeper modeling did not pull conversion up.
The Signals · 05
Correlation, plotted directly

r ≈ 0.24 — not significant.

  • Pearson r ≈ 0.24 at n=13. The p=0.05 threshold for this sample is ~0.55 — we are nowhere close.
  • r² ≈ 0.06 — modeling explains only 6% of the variance in conversion.
  • Across the full range (11%–58% modeling), predicted conversion moves only 1.96% → 2.01%.
The Signals · 06
What this means

The 2% is structural, not an activation-depth problem.

Confirmed
Activation is weak
Import Usage stuck at a ~40% ceiling — the Module 1 hypothesis about a leaky activation layer holds.
Sharpened
But pushing depth doesn't move conversion
Modeling usage rose to 2× its base and conversion didn't budge. The 2% is a structural ceiling — pricing, paywall timing, or packaging.
Split
Two fronts, not one
(a) Lift Import Usage to grow the North Star pool. (b) Run a separate pricing/paywall experiment — sets up Module 6.
The Signals · 07
Where to experiment next

Two experiments, not one.

  • Experiment A — Activation: remove the connect-a-bank wall (the "Sample-Data On-Ramp") to lift Import Usage from 40% toward 60%+. This is Module 5.
  • Experiment B — Structural conversion: test pricing, paywall timing, or packaging to break the 2% ceiling. This is Module 6.
  • Why both: growing the pool (A) fills a bucket that still leaks (B). Neither alone is enough.
